The Group

A Force That Changed Everything

BIGBANG came from YG Entertainment in 2006. They were five men, with a really cool idea. They wanted to mix hip-hop and R&B and pop and electronic music to make something totally new. BIGBANG wanted to create music that the world had never heard before.

For twenty years the band members of this group did some amazing things. They broke lots of records. They got fans around the world to love them. Korean pop music is really popular. They helped make it that way. They also helped people from cultures like the same music. The band members made some great music that people will always remember. At the time each member of the group also had a very successful music career on their own. They are all very famous for the music they made with the group and for the music they made by themselves. The Korean pop group shattered records. They built a big following of fans all around the world. The Korean pop group also influenced what people wear. They helped people from different cultures, like each other. The Korean pop group made some great music and each member of the Korean pop group had a very successful solo music career.

G-Dragon, Taeyang, Daesung, T.O.P and Seungri did not just become stars. They became important parts of pop culture. Their impact still affects every group of K-Pop fans and artists that comes out. Their music and style have inspired many. Today young K-Pop stars look up to them. G-Dragon, Taeyang, Daesung, T.O.P and Seungri were truly game-changers, in K-Pop.

Inside the Hallyu Wave

K-Pop did not become popular worldwide in one day. It took a lot of effort, planning and trust that music from Korea could be loved by people
The groups trained for hours long before they became famous.
The companies created programs to help young trainees become performers who could sing, dance and act all, at once.
This is the world BIGBANG came from. Its still growing today with K-Pop groups and music making waves globally and K-Pop fans loving the music.

Fandom Culture

Lightsticks & Loyalty

Every K-Pop fandom has its own name, its own color, and its own lightstick, and waving one at a concert is like being part of one huge, glowing crowd. Fans learn fan chants by heart, cheer specific member names between song lines, and treat a concert like a reunion instead of just a show.

Fandoms also take care of each other. Long after cameras stop rolling, fans keep sharing old performances, translating interviews, and welcoming new fans who are just discovering a group for the first time. It is one of the reasons K-Pop fan communities can stay so close, even across countries and years.

The Chant — fans call out member names between lines, live, every show.
The Glow — a sea of lightsticks turns a stadium into one shared color.
The Vote — fandoms organize online to push a comeback up the charts.
The Welcome — veteran fans translate and explain for anyone just joining in.
